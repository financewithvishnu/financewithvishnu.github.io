83.1What are the time value of money formulas?

These are the compounding and discounting identities: how money grows forward through interest, and how future sums translate back to today’s value. Every loan, SIP and valuation in this website reduces to these.

QuantityFormulaSymbols / notes
Future value (lump sum)FV = P x (1 + r)^nP principal, r per-period rate, n periods
Present valuePV = F / (1 + r)^nF future amount
FV of a SIP (annuity)FV = A x [((1 + i)^n - 1) / i] x (1 + i)A instalment, i per-period rate; x(1+i) for month-start SIPs

83.3What are the bond pricing and duration formulas?

These formulas price a bond from its cash flows and measure how far its price moves when yields change. Duration is the single most useful risk number in fixed income.

83.6How should you use this formula sheet?

Three habits make the sheet earn its place. Check the assumptions before the arithmetic: CAGR assumes one flow, YTM assumes reinvestment at the yield, Sharpe assumes roughly symmetric returns, and the chapters listed beside each table carry the failure modes. Keep units consistent, monthly rates with monthly counts, real with real, most formula errors are unit errors wearing arithmetic's clothes. And sanity-check every output against an anchor you already own: doubling times, the 4% rule's 25x, the Chapter 78 retirement arithmetic. A formula is a lever; anchors are the ground it pushes against.
