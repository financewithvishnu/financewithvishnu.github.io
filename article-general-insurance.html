General insurance: the premium arrives before the final bill

By Finance With Vishnu · Source period: August 2021

An insurer receives money for a promise. The customer knows the premium, but the insurer may not know the final cost of that promise for years. This creates both the investment opportunity and the accounting risk.

Premium growth, cash generation and reported profit can all look healthy while the underlying book deteriorates. I would therefore study the promises, their eventual cost and the capital supporting them before looking at the investment portfolio.

The central question is whether underwriting, reserves and investments together create cash that shareholders can safely receive. A large asset pool is not the same thing as a large pool of money belonging to shareholders.

Source period: August 2021. FY2025 filings, Berkshire's 2024 report and a 2024 regulatory circular are later historical cross-checks, not information assumed available in that source period. All numerical examples below are original simplified hypotheticals. This is an educational business study, not a policy recommendation, claims guide, current regulatory opinion or stock valuation.

1. What is an insurer actually selling?

Insurance transfers specified financial risk from the customer to a pool, subject to the policy's terms. That pool only works economically if the premium covers expected claims, acquisition and administration, uncertainty and the cost of capital.

Large numbers do not rescue inadequate pricing. Suppose 1,000 customers each pay ₹6,000. The pool collects ₹60 lakh. If six covered claims cost ₹10 lakh each, claims alone consume the whole premium. Distribution, staff and capital still need paying. Diversification reduces some uncertainty; it does not make expected costs disappear.

Frequency is how often claims occur. Severity is their average cost. Correlation is the possibility that many exposures suffer together, as in a flood or pandemic. Better data on ordinary claims does not automatically establish protection against a rare correlated event.

Start with the contract: covered event, limits, deductible, exclusions, geography and settlement obligation. Then ask what risks are retained, transferred to reinsurers or left with customers. Premium is the price of a particular promise, not a uniform unit of economic output.

2. Why do different insurance lines need different models?

Motor own damage covers specified damage to the insured vehicle. Motor third-party liability concerns specified obligations to others. Health, property, marine, engineering and crop each have different exposure, pricing, settlement and concentration patterns.

A policy's coverage period is not its claim tail. A one-year contract can generate liability claims settled many years later. A shorter-tail line may reveal pricing mistakes faster, but can still carry reporting delays and reserve uncertainty.

In motor, separate vehicle numbers, insured values, repair costs, renewals and liability development. In health, distinguish retail, employer/group and government business, benefit design, covered lives, medical inflation and hospital arrangements. In property or crop, examine catastrophe concentration and recovery terms.

ICICI Lombard's FY2025 report illustrates a diversified general-insurance model, while Star Health's FY2025 report illustrates a more focused health model. They are useful contrasting disclosure sets, not interchangeable premium multiples. Their definitions and reporting bases must be reconciled before comparison. [1, 2]

Life insurance has a different product and valuation structure. A framework built around life embedded value should not simply be pasted onto a general insurer.

3. Which premium number are we looking at?

Gross written premium is measured before ceded reinsurance. Net written premium reflects the retained amount on the relevant reporting basis. Net earned premium reflects coverage supplied during the period, rather than merely contracts booked or cash collected.

The bridge assumes consistent net definitions and no other adjustments. Actual company policies and product rules can require further reconciliation. An unearned-premium reserve is a liability for unexpired coverage, not equity accumulated for owners.

A simple annual policy beginning halfway through a financial year might earn half its premium by year-end if risk is uniform over the coverage period. That illustration is not a universal earning rule for every insurance product.

The historical 2016 IRDAI regulations define unearned premium and premium-deficiency reserves separately. The latter addresses expected costs exceeding the provision for unexpired coverage. Later reporting changes are another reason not to extend an old premium table mechanically. [3]

4. Why are claims paid not the same as claims incurred?

An event can happen before the insurer hears about it. A notified claim can then be revised, reopened or litigated before settlement. The accounts need estimates of these obligations, rather than waiting for the final cheque.

Reported outstanding reserves cover claims already notified but not fully settled. Incurred-but-not-reported reserves address additional obligations under the applicable actuarial definition. In the 2016 IRDAI framework, IBNR includes IBNER, or expected changes in estimates of reported claims, as well as specified other components. These labels are not mutually exclusive buckets that can be added without reading the definition. [3]

Keep paid amounts, reserves and recoveries consistently gross or net of reinsurance. The 2024 regulatory circular separately reinforces that IBNR must be estimated using sound actuarial principles and treated as claims liability. [4]

A reserve estimate is uncertain, but it is not optional. Calling a strengthening charge non-cash does not make the underlying obligation harmless.

5. What does a combined ratio really measure?

The loss ratio compares incurred claims with earned premium on the stated basis. The combined ratio adds the relevant acquisition and operating-cost ratios. The denominator, treatment of reinsurance commission and expense allocation need checking.

On a single earned-premium basis, a combined ratio below 100% indicates underwriting profit before investment income. But some disclosed conventions use written premium for expense ratios. Their sum does not necessarily reconcile by multiplying the combined ratio by earned premium.

Continue the example: earned premium is 700, incurred claims are 490 and underwriting expenses are 160. Underwriting profit is 50. Total costs divided by earned premium give 650/700, or 92.86%.

If the expense ratio instead uses net written premium of 800, it is 20%. Adding that to the 70% claims ratio gives 90%, even though actual underwriting profit is 50, not 70. Neither ratio should be silently relabelled. Read the published convention and the revenue account together.

On a consistent basis, small changes matter. With earned premium of 1,000, each percentage point of total underwriting cost is 10 of result. Technology savings help, but a modest error in claims severity can overwhelm them.

6. Can profit improve while new underwriting gets worse?

Yes. Older claims may develop more favourably than estimated, releasing reserves into the current result. Adverse development does the opposite. Separate the current accident-year experience from changes relating to earlier years.

A reserving triangle follows a cohort across successive valuation dates. Confirm whether the table shows paid, incurred or ultimate claims, and whether figures are cumulative or incremental. Compare the same accident year as it matures, rather than comparing young and mature cohorts as though they have equal information.

ICICI Lombard's FY2025 report refers to its history of publishing reserving triangles. That is useful because transparency allows a reader to test earlier estimates; disclosure alone does not prove that all future estimates will be adequate. [1]

Favourable development can indicate earlier conservatism, but can also coexist with underpriced new business. Changes in product mix, inflation, litigation, catastrophe experience and actuarial method affect comparisons.

I would ask how much of today's profit came from earlier assumptions, whether long-tail cohorts are mature enough to judge and what happens if the latest estimates need strengthening.

7. Is float free money?

Float is funding created by receiving premiums before all related claims and expenses are paid. The insurer can invest assets supporting those obligations, subject to liquidity, investment and capital constraints. It does not thereby own the liabilities as equity.

Underwriting profit can make this funding economically attractive. Persistent underwriting losses can make it expensive. A longer settlement tail offers more investment time but adds uncertainty about inflation, legal outcomes and ultimate claim amounts.

Berkshire's 2024 report explains the cash-before-loss-payments model and the danger that long-tail mistakes can remain hidden. Its experience is a conceptual cross-check, not a return template or an Indian regulatory comparison. [5]

Growth can increase cash even when policies are underpriced. A slowing book may stop providing fresh premium while old claims continue to mature. Test the cash schedule rather than treating positive operating cash flow as free cash flow.

8. What does reinsurance change?

Reinsurance transfers specified risk in return for premium and contractual terms. It can reduce concentration and protect capital, but introduces counterparty, coverage and collection risk.

A proportional quota-share arrangement shares specified premium and claims. Excess-of-loss protection responds after losses cross an attachment, up to a limit. Ceding 20% of premium does not universally mean recovering 20% of every claim.

Examine exclusions, aggregate limits, reinstatement terms, recoverables, collateral where relevant and timing. A strong-looking gross portfolio can leave a different retained book after reinsurance. Compare gross and net product mixes.

Changing a treaty can alter commission income, earning patterns, reserves, capital needs and retained future losses. It is unsafe to dismiss every transition effect as mere accounting. Equally, a temporary adjustment should not be extrapolated as a permanent annual cost without understanding it.

The 2024 circular's treatment of overdue reinsurer and co-insurer balances also shows why recoverables require attention in solvency analysis. A receivable is not equivalent to cash available today. [4]

9. Does a large investment book prove solvency?

No. Investments divided by written premium is not the solvency ratio. It ignores liabilities and regulatory adjustments.

In the historical 2016 framework, the solvency ratio is available solvency margin divided by required solvency margin. Available margin starts from admissible assets less liabilities with prescribed adjustments; required margin follows the prescribed calculations. That framework specified a 150% control level. This is a dated regulatory reference, not an assertion that every old provision is today's complete rulebook. [3]

A profitable insurer may need new equity when growth consumes capital. A higher buffer can reduce near-term ROE while improving resilience. Neither maximum leverage nor idle capital is automatically the best outcome.

Track distributions, dilution, reserve stress and capital calls together. Point-in-time strength is not a permanent promise.

10. What makes distribution and service an advantage?

Agents, brokers, banks, direct channels and digital platforms can bring customers. They also take commissions, require investment and influence the risk mix. A low acquisition cost is useful only if the retained customer economics remain sound.

Renewal premium can rise because prices or cover increase while some customers leave. Renewal by premium value, policy count and customer count are different metrics. Reinsurance retention is different again.

In health, a hospital network can improve access and administration, but its size alone does not prove pricing power or fair service. Track settlement experience, complaints, processing time and product terms on comparable definitions. A low claims ratio achieved by poor service can damage the franchise.

Retail business may have different renewal and pricing characteristics from negotiated group business. It is not inherently well priced. Medical inflation is simultaneously a reason customers need cover and a cost the insurer must manage.

Digital issuance or automated claims processing should earn its place in the model through lower costs, better selection or customer retention. An app-download count is not proof of an underwriting advantage.

11. Which insurance-adjacent business does the share own?

A third-party administrator provides services; it is not the same underwriting risk-taker as an insurer. Medi Assist's FY2025 release describes facilitation among insurers, policyholders, providers and public-scheme beneficiaries, and separately reports premium under management and operating revenue. Administered premium is not its own revenue or insurance float. [6]

For an administrator, study fee rates, client concentration, staff productivity, service obligations, technology spending, acquisitions and cash conversion. Large transaction volume can coexist with weak bargaining power.

A holding company creates another distinction. If it owns 60% of an insurance subsidiary valued hypothetically at 1,000, its gross interest is 600. Parent debt, tax, other businesses, minority rights, capital needs and access to distributions still matter.

Build the entity tree before applying a multiple. Do not combine life, general insurance and lending metrics or value the entire subsidiary as though the listed parent owned all of it.

12. Why is the investment portfolio not a valuation floor?

An insurer that stops writing business still owes unexpired cover, notified claims, IBNR, settlement expenses and other liabilities. It may need capital for years during run-off. Subtracting one year's claims from investments does not identify surplus shareholder capital.

A valid run-off model needs all obligations, their settlement schedule, asset realisation, tax, expenses and capital release. The investment base shrinks as claims are paid. Capitalising income on a static residual forever can overstate a finite run-off.

Higher interest rates can improve future reinvestment yield while reducing the value of existing bonds. Duration, liquidity, credit risk, realisation policy and claims inflation determine the net effect. There is no universal percentage-point ROE benefit from a rate increase.

Book value depends on credible liabilities and asset values. Earnings can contain reserve releases and realised investment gains. A premium multiple ignores margin and capital intensity. Each shortcut needs a bridge back to sustainable distributable earnings.

I would forecast underwriting and investments separately, deduct tax and incremental equity needs, and discount plausible shareholder distributions. A good business can still be a poor purchase if its price assumes unusually benign claims and investment conditions.

13. What belongs on the final research sheet?

Begin with exposure and product mix, not one premium-growth target. Reconcile written to earned premium, paid to incurred claims and underwriting to final profit.

Keep a consistent record of reserve development, gross/net reinsurance, normalised investment yield, asset quality, liquidity, solvency, dilution and per-share equity. Separate accident-year performance from releases and one-offs.

Stress higher frequency and severity, a catastrophe, slower renewal, weaker investment returns, overdue recoveries and reserve strengthening. Premium inflation may merely compensate for claims inflation. Rapid market-share gains may reflect pricing that competitors wisely refused.

The best sign is the ability to reject uneconomic business while serving valid claims fairly and retaining enough capital. The final test is whether the insurer can honour its promises through an adverse period and still earn an adequate return for owners. Everything else, including the size of its float, follows from that.
