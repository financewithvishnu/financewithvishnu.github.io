Chapter 43. Government Securities and the Yield Curve

Chapter 43 of 85 · about 20 minutes

Every interest rate in your financial life, your home loan, your FD, your bond fund, your company's cost of debt, is priced off one foundation: what the Government of India pays to borrow. That foundation has a shape, the yield curve, and the shape is one of the most reliable economic signals ever recorded. This chapter teaches you the instruments, the shape, and how an ordinary investor can now hold the foundation directly.

43.1What are treasury bills, dated securities and state development loans?

The government's borrowing shelf has three levels. Treasury bills are the short end: 91, 182 and 364-day paper sold at a discount to face value, the closest thing in India to pure cash. Dated securities, the famous G-secs, are the long end: fixed-coupon bonds from 2 to 40 years, auctioned by the RBI on the government's behalf every week; the 10-year's yield is the number the entire market watches. State development loans are the states' own bonds, paying a small premium over the centre's for a small amount of extra risk and a lot less liquidity. All three are sovereign promises in rupees, which makes them the definition of default-safe in India, and, per Chapter 42, not remotely price-safe at the long end.

43.2What is the yield curve, and what does each shape predict?

Plot the government's borrowing cost against maturity and you get the yield curve, the bond market's collective economic forecast drawn as a line. The normal shape slopes up: longer money costs more, reflecting growth, inflation risk and the price of waiting. The flat shape says the market expects the present not to last. The inverted shape, short rates above long, says the central bank is braking hard now and the market expects it to be cutting later, because a slowdown is coming; inversion has preceded nearly every modern recession across countries, India's shallow inversions included, not as magic but as logic: the bond market is voting that today's tight money cannot be sustained. The chart shows all three. Reading the curve will not tell you when; it reliably tells you which way the pressure builds.

43.3What does a sovereign rating mean, and what is risk-free?

India's sovereign rating sits at the bottom rung of investment grade, a verdict that mixes genuine strengths, a democracy that has never defaulted on its rupee debt, deep domestic savings, with genuine constraints, high public debt, low per-capita income, a narrow tax base. "Risk-free" therefore means something precise and narrow: the government prints the currency the debt is denominated in, so outright rupee default is near-unthinkable; the risks that remain are inflation, which taxes every fixed coupon quietly, and price risk, Chapter 42's seesaw. Foreign-currency sovereign debt is a different, genuinely riskier animal, as Sri Lanka's 2022 default reminded the neighbourhood. Hold the distinction: rupee G-secs are default-safe and purchasing-power-risky; that second risk is the one that actually reaches you.

43.4How do you buy government securities in India?

Since 2021, any Indian resident can open an RBI Retail Direct account and buy government securities at the same weekly auctions as the institutions, no intermediary, no fund fee, face values from ₹10,000. The catch is the one Chapter 42 taught: a directly held 30-year G-sec is default-safe but its market price swings, and selling early means taking the seesaw's current seat. The gentler routes: gilt funds, which hold the same paper with daily liquidity and a small fee, and target-maturity funds, index-style debt funds that hold bonds to a fixed maturity date, giving you something close to a direct bond's certainty with a fund's convenience. The choice map: hold to maturity and want zero fees, Retail Direct; want liquidity and simplicity, gilt funds; want a known maturity with fund convenience, target-maturity funds.

43.5Why are US Treasuries the world’s benchmark?

The world's yield curve is America's. The US Treasury market is the deepest pool of safe money on earth, its 10-year yield the discount rate inside every global valuation, your Indian stocks included: when Treasury yields jump, the world's cost of equity rises, foreign flows leave emerging markets, and the Nifty feels a meeting held in Washington. The dollar's reserve status lets the US borrow what no one else can, and every emerging-market crisis of the modern era has had a Treasury-yield spike in its opening scene. You do not need to trade Treasuries; you need to know that their yield is the tide level in which all other boats, including Indian G-secs, float.

43.6Worked examples and cases

Case 1: reading an inverted curve before a slowdown

The 2019 rehearsal, pattern repeated worldwide in 2022-23: the central bank has hiked through a year of inflation, short rates have climbed past long rates, and the curve inverts. The commentary divides into "this time is different" and "the signal has spoken". The disciplined reading is narrower and more useful: inversion says the bond market expects cuts, and cuts come when the economy weakens; it does not say the market falls tomorrow, equities often rally for months after inversion, but it raises the probability that the next two years contain a slowdown, and it argues, concretely, for locking long yields while they exist and for holding the portfolio that survives Chapter 18's act four. The curve is a weather report. You do not cancel the picnic on a forecast; you do pack the umbrella.

Case 2: building a G-sec ladder

The construction for an investor who wants sovereign certainty without rate-timing, illustrative numbers via Retail Direct:

RungInstrument (illustrative)YieldMatures
1182-day T-bill6.8%6 months
22-year G-sec7.0%2 years
35-year G-sec7.1%5 years
410-year G-sec7.2%10 years
As each rung matures, reinvest at the then-current long end: the ladder rolls, and rate timing stops being your problem.

The ladder's quiet genius: some rung is always maturing, so reinvestment happens at whatever rates then prevail, high rates get locked, low rates touch only one rung, and the investor never once has to answer "where are rates going?". Duration is matched to the actual spending horizon, the coupons arrive as scheduled income, and Chapter 42's seesaw becomes scenery rather than danger. It is the fixed-income version of the SIP: a machine that converts a forecasting problem into a habit.
