NBFCs: the lending edge is not the loan yield

By Finance With Vishnu · Source period: June 2023

A lender charging a higher interest rate is not necessarily running a better business. The rate may be paying for expensive funding, costly collections, uncertain borrower income or losses that have not appeared yet.

For a non-banking financial company, or NBFC, I would start with a different question: what can this lender understand or do better than a bank, and can it keep that advantage after funding costs, operating expenses and credit losses?

The answer might be local borrower knowledge, fast service, a specialised asset, distribution or repeat customer relationships. But a lending edge must survive collection day. Disbursing more money is the easy part of the story.

1. Why should this lender exist?

Cheap money is a powerful advantage, but it is not the only lending skill. A bank may find a small, irregular-income borrower expensive to assess and service. An NBFC might build a repeatable process around that customer's business, geography or asset.

The economic niche matters more than a broad sector label. A vehicle operator, a small shop owner pledging property, a salaried consumer buying an appliance and a developer seeking project finance have different cash flows and failure modes.

Separate the borrower, the purpose of the loan and the collateral. A business loan secured by a house is not the same product as a loan to purchase that house. The security tells us something about possible recovery, not everything about repayment capacity.

Cholamandalam's FY2025 report distinguishes vehicle finance, loans against property and home loans. Five-Star's report discusses small-ticket secured lending and borrower-level risk assessment. These are useful examples of why product and customer maps come before a peer ranking. [2, 3]

2. Is the funding advantage real?

NBFCs do not have the same demand-deposit franchise as banks. That does not mean every NBFC is prohibited from accepting every deposit. RBI distinguishes deposit-taking and non-deposit-taking entities; specific permission matters. Its FAQ also makes clear that NBFC deposits do not have bank-deposit insurance. [5]

Study bank loans, bonds, commercial paper, permitted deposits, refinance, foreign borrowing and asset transfers separately. A low headline coupon on foreign debt says little without currency hedging, fees, maturity and refinancing conditions.

Funding quality includes concentration. Several facilities from lenders exposed to the same market shock may be less diversified than the number of names suggests. Parent reputation is useful context, but not a substitute for legally committed support.

Bajaj Finance's FY2025 discussion identifies deposits, borrowings, liquidity buffers and fully hedged external borrowing. Five-Star describes a different borrowing mix and identifies itself as non-deposit-taking in that reporting year. These are dated entity-specific disclosures, not permission to infer the status of every lender today. [1, 3]

3. Can a profitable lender run short of cash?

Yes. Profitability, solvency and liquidity answer different questions. A performing loan due over several years cannot automatically repay a borrowing that matures next week.

Asset-liability management, or ALM, compares expected cash inflows with outflows across time. Read the assumptions behind collections, prepayments, renewals and liquid assets. A comfortable-looking cumulative bucket does not prove that cash will arrive exactly as forecast.

A liquidity buffer and committed lines can help, subject to their actual availability and conditions. Selling assets may also provide cash, but the price, execution time and retained obligations matter.

Five-Star's FY2025 report discusses the tenure of fresh borrowings and securitisation as part of its funding approach. Bajaj Finance separately reports a closing liquidity buffer. Those disclosures point to different dimensions of liquidity, not one interchangeable safety ratio. [3, 1]

4. What survives after the headline yield?

Loan yield is not shareholder return. Funding costs apply to the interest-bearing liabilities used, while yield applies to the relevant earning assets. Equity, cash, other assets and different average balances prevent a casual subtraction from becoming a complete profit model.

The example omits many real reporting details deliberately. It shows why consistent balances are necessary. It does not establish a reported NIM, ROA or ROE without the appropriate average assets and equity.

Also distinguish average cost of funds during a year from the exit rate at its end. Bajaj Finance discloses both in its FY2025 discussion. A lower exit rate does not mean the full year's funding enjoyed that rate. [1]

5. Is a low cost-to-income ratio always good?

Efficient branches, repeat borrowers and useful technology can spread costs over more activity. But underwriting, fraud checks, borrower service and collections are necessary work, not expenses that can disappear without consequence.

Inspect staff turnover, branch maturity, collection capacity and complaint handling alongside productivity. A new district may need more time and supervision than a mature one. Growth can expose weaknesses before the cost ratio reveals them.

Digital processing can reduce friction, but an automated decision is still a credit decision. Better data must lead to better measured outcomes, not merely faster approvals. Check fraud, losses, repeat use and customer affordability by vintage.

Chola's FY2025 report discusses common service and collection systems. UGRO describes data-led underwriting. Both are company descriptions of capabilities; neither statement alone independently proves superior prediction or loss control. [2, 4]

6. Why can secured lending still lose money?

Repayment normally comes from borrower cash flow. Collateral is a possible second route to recovery, with valuation, title, custody, legal process and time risk.

A truck can lose resale value when transport conditions weaken. Property can be difficult to sell just when many borrowers in the same locality need relief. A large number of small loans can share one economic shock.

Look at geography, industry, income source, collateral type, collection channel and loan age separately. A portfolio may be granular by ticket size yet concentrated in one region or occupation.

Five-Star's FY2025 report describes borrower leverage and loan purpose among pricing inputs and reports deterioration in asset-quality measures despite secured lending. The analytical lesson is not that collateral is useless. It is that security and timely repayment are different protections. [3]

7. Are ECL stages the same as overdue buckets?

No. Expected credit loss, or ECL, is a forward-looking impairment framework. Overdue and regulatory asset-classification measures serve related but different purposes. Do not rename every early-overdue bucket Stage 1 or assume Stage 3 and GNPA always match exactly.

The IFRS Foundation's explanation of the general impairment approach distinguishes 12-month expected losses from lifetime expected losses when credit risk has increased significantly. Credit-impaired assets form a further category. This explains the underlying concept, not the detailed Indian applicability or implementation for every lender. [6]

Twelve-month ECL is not simply the next twelve months of missed instalments. It concerns expected lifetime cash shortfalls associated with defaults possible over that horizon. Models also require assumptions about exposure, default probability, loss severity and scenarios.

Read each company's accounting policy, staging criteria, overlays and movement tables. Compare provisions with actual recoveries and write-offs over time. A precise model output can still depend on uncertain assumptions.

Fast growth can dilute a closing bad-loan ratio with new loans that have not seasoned. Track earlier origination cohorts through time before concluding that underwriting has improved.

8. What changes under co-lending?

In a co-lending arrangement, partners participate in lending under the applicable agreement and rules. One party may contribute specialist origination and servicing while another supplies a larger funding share.

That can reduce the NBFC's own funded exposure relative to the total customer pool it serves. But customer acquisition, underwriting, servicing, collections and partner management still cost money. Capital requirements and retained risk do not become zero.

Do not multiply a fee by total AUM and divide by a tiny equity number without reconciling costs and risk. A partner can also change appetite, pricing or renewal terms, creating concentration and execution risk.

UGRO's FY2025 report separately highlights total AUM and an off-book co-lending share. That is a reason to reconcile managed and own-book measures before comparing it with a lender retaining most loans. [4]

9. How do assignment and securitisation differ?

A direct assignment transfers eligible receivables under an agreement. Securitisation structures a pool's cash flows through securities, commonly with a special-purpose vehicle. Co-lending is a shared-lending arrangement rather than simply another name for selling an existing pool.

For each structure, ask what is transferred, what remains and who bears losses. Retained interests, credit enhancement, representations, servicing duties and accounting derecognition can change the answer.

Asset transfer is not inherently a disposal of bad loans. Five-Star's FY2025 report describes securitisation of receivables as a funding activity. Do not infer that every transferred pool is distressed or that no exposure remains. [3]

Separate upfront gains from recurring servicing income and cash collection. Growth funded by transfers can be sound, but reported revenue and ROA may not be directly comparable with an entirely on-book model. Read the accounting notes and pool-level risks.

This article deliberately avoids universal holding periods, minimum shares or guarantee caps. Such conditions need the applicable rules and transaction documents, not a remembered classroom illustration.

10. Does high ROA prove superior economics?

Not by itself. A lender using more equity pays interest on less debt relative to assets. That can lift accounting ROA while leaving ROE less exceptional. Equity has an economic cost even though ordinary dividends are not an interest expense.

The second lender is not automatically better. Its higher leverage makes losses more consequential for equity. The first is not automatically inefficient: a larger capital buffer may reflect deliberate resilience or a young growth phase.

ROE divided by ROA only approximates assets/equity when profits, periods and average denominators match. It does not directly give borrowings/equity. A ratio using closing loan AUM is not automatically comparable with one using average total assets.

More leverage cannot create a good lending opportunity. It helps returns only if the additional business earns enough after its full costs and risks.

11. Why do broad-sector scorecards mislead?

A gold lender, card issuer, vehicle financier and small-business property lender should not be ranked as though their yields, collection costs and loss behaviour were interchangeable.

First compare same-product peers, then examine the broader franchise. A high yield can be counted positively while the associated credit cost is counted negatively, although both reflect the same underlying risk choice.

Likewise, funding cost, NIM, ROA, ROE and leverage are connected. Giving each an equal rank does not make the final score neutral. Rank sums also discard magnitude: a tiny difference and a large one can move a company by the same position.

Use a scorecard as an organised set of questions. Document definitions, uncertainties and weights. If a small change in one assumption reverses the conclusion, the ranking is fragile rather than precise.

Keep governance, incentives, related parties, customer conduct and funding stress outside the temptation to reduce everything to one number.

12. How does a good business become a poor investment?

The price can assume more growth and durability than the business delivers. Even rising book value per share can coexist with a disappointing share-price return if the valuation multiple falls.

For an original illustration, five years of 15% annual book-value-per-share growth give about twice the starting book value. If the market multiple falls from four times book to two times, the ending price is almost unchanged before dividends, taxes and costs.

This is not a forecast or a rule that a particular multiple is always too high. Sustainable returns, capital needs, growth, risk and the credibility of reported book value all matter.

Watch dilution as well as total AUM and profit. New capital can support a better business while changing each existing share's claim. An acquisition or asset sale also needs its own cash, debt, tax and minority-interest bridge.

My final research sheet would connect the niche, liabilities, liquidity, collections, loss recognition, own-book versus managed assets, sustainable returns and valuation. The best-looking yield is only the beginning of that work.
