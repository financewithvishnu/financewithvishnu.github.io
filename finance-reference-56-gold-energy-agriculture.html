Part VIII · Commodities: Indian Markets and Strategies

Chapter 56. Gold, Energy and Agriculture

Chapter 56 of 85 · about 20 minutes

Four commodity families matter to an Indian investor: the gold in the locker, the crude in the import bill, the crops in the mandi, and the metals in the factories. Each prices a different force, fear, geopolitics, weather and industry, and each teaches a different lesson. This chapter walks the four, ending with two of the great commodity stories of the modern era.

56.1Why is gold a monetary commodity, and what is India’s special relationship with it?

Gold is the odd commodity: barely consumed, mostly hoarded, paying nothing, priced by the two forces that have priced it for five thousand years, real interest rates and fear. When real yields fall, the cost of holding a bar of nothing falls with them; when the world frightens, the oldest insurance policy gets bid. Its honest portfolio role is diversifier and disaster hedge, not growth engine. India adds a cultural layer no model captures: the country imports hundreds of tonnes a year, and Indian households sit on what is often estimated as the world's largest private hoard, jewellery that is simultaneously savings, insurance, dowry and devotion. For the investor this cuts both ways. The demand is durable and price-insensitive, a floor of sorts; and the national habit of saving in an unproductive asset is a real economic cost, which is why the government keeps engineering paper alternatives, gold ETFs, and the sovereign gold bonds of Chapter 57, that pay interest on the country's oldest savings habit.

56.2What moves crude oil and natural gas prices?

Crude is the commodity that taxes everything else. Its price is set by a cartel's discipline (OPEC and its Russian appendix), a swing producer's drill rigs (American shale), and the periodic arrival of war, embargoes and blockades, with demand riding the global industrial cycle. For India the price is a macro variable, not a trade idea: the country imports the overwhelming bulk of its crude, so every ten dollars on Brent widens the import bill, pressures the rupee, feeds inflation, and lands in the RBI's reaction function. Natural gas, regional rather than global in pricing, follows its own logistics of pipelines and LNG tankers, as Europe re-learned in 2022. The investor's takeaways: energy prices are policy inputs in India, watched by the central bank and the finance ministry as closely as by traders; energy futures are professional instruments with violent roll dynamics; and the cleanest energy exposure for a portfolio is through the businesses, refiners, explorers, distributors, rather than the barrel itself.

56.3How do seasons, monsoons and mandis drive agricultural prices?

Agricultural prices are written by the sky and then edited by the state. The monsoon sets the supply; the crop calendar sets the rhythm, sowing, arrivals, lean season; and the government overlays the floor (minimum support prices), the plumbing (mandis and procurement), and the switches, export bans, stock limits, import duty changes, that can reprice a crop overnight. Futures on NCDEX exist to let farmers, traders and processors manage exactly this risk, but the market lives under a permanent shadow: when prices of essentials spike, the political response has repeatedly been to suspend futures trading in the offending commodity, as happened to several contracts in the 2021-22 inflation scare, whatever the evidence that futures smooth rather than cause the spikes. The investor's lesson: agricultural commodities carry an extra risk no chart shows, regulatory weather, and their futures are tools for those inside the farm economy more than instruments for outside portfolios.

56.4Why is copper called the industrial cycle in one chart?

Copper is called Dr. Copper because it diagnoses the world economy without an opinion: wired into construction, power, transport and now every electric vehicle and grid upgrade, its price is the industrial cycle's thermometer. Aluminium, zinc, lead and nickel follow the same logic with their own supply quirks, smelter economics, energy costs, the occasional exchange drama, as nickel's 2022 short squeeze on the LME demonstrated when prices went vertical and the exchange cancelled trades, a reminder that even benchmark markets have operators with discretion. Two features define the family for investors. Demand concentrates: China's construction and manufacturing cycle has been the single largest driver for three decades, so trading copper is in large part trading China. And supply responds slowly: a mine takes a decade from discovery to metal, so the cycle runs longer and meaner than in financial assets, gluts and squeezes measured in years.

56.5Worked examples and cases

Case 1: negative oil, April 2020

On 20 April 2020, the May WTI futures contract settled at minus $37 a barrel: sellers paid buyers to take oil away. The mechanics were Chapter 55 in action. The contract required physical delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma; the pandemic had cut demand by a fifth; Cushing's storage was effectively full; and financial longs who had never intended to own a barrel discovered, in the contract's last days, that there was nobody left to sell to who could actually take delivery. The price fell through zero, the market's way of saying that oil with nowhere to go is not an asset but a liability. The lessons are permanent. A futures contract is a promise with a delivery clause, and the clause matters most exactly when everyone has ignored it longest. Commodity prices can do things financial theory says are impossible, because physical constraints, storage, transport, perishability, do not appear in the equations. And anyone holding a commodity position near expiry without a plan for the physical reality is, as the phrase went, one door knock away from owning a tanker.

Case 2: gold vs equity across Indian decades

The chart shows the two great Indian savings assets, indexed to 100 in 1995, illustrative paths rather than precise history. Equity compounded faster across the full span, the Nifty roughly doubling gold's multiple over three decades, and that is the expected outcome: businesses earn, gold waits. But the path matters as much as the endpoint. Gold won entire decades, the 2000s spectacularly, as the dollar gold bull ran and the rupee's gentle slide added a tailwind to every INR-denominated bar. And in the crisis years, 2008, 2011, 2020, gold's line rises precisely when equity's falls, which is the property a portfolio actually buys. The Indian investor's synthesis, matching Chapter 57's allocation guidance: gold is not competition for equity but its complement, a 5-15% allocation that lowers the whole portfolio's blood pressure, earns its keep in the decades when everything else disappoints, and should be held in its interest-bearing paper forms rather than the locker, where it earns nothing and insures only against burglary.
