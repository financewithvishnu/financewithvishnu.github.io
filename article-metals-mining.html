Metals and mining: separate the commodity from the conversion

By Finance With Vishnu · Source period: February 2024

A miner, a steelmaker and a manufacturer of specialised wire ropes can all appear in the metals sector. They do not sell the same thing. One sells a mineral. Another transforms raw materials into metal. A third turns that metal into a product designed for a customer's application.

I would first ask where the company earns its spread. Is it exposed mainly to the selling price of a commodity, the cost of processing it, or the value of an approved product and service? The answer changes what needs to be monitored.

Value addition can make earnings more durable, but the word specialised is not enough. We still need to understand volumes, input costs, pricing terms, capital employed and cash collection. A protected conversion spread can coexist with a severe fall in customer demand.

1. Where does the business sit in the chain?

A useful first map separates mining, preparation of mineral inputs, metal production and downstream conversion. Some companies own several stages. Others buy their main input from outside.

Worldsteel's process map distinguishes raw materials, ironmaking, steelmaking, semi-finished forms and subsequent products. Slabs, billets and blooms are intermediate forms, not an interchangeable measure of finished customer demand. [1]

I would draw the company's actual chain, mark what is captive and show what is bought or sold externally. That helps prevent an integrated business being valued as though every stage contributes independent revenue on the same tonne.

2. Why does the steelmaking route matter?

The production route determines the relevant inputs, assets and cost sensitivities. A blast-furnace and basic-oxygen route is different from an electric-arc route using suitable metallic inputs. Direct-reduced iron is an intermediate input, not finished steel.

Worldsteel identifies two main steelmaking routes and shows a range of raw materials, including iron ore, coal, scrap, natural gas and alternative fuels. A generic statement that all steelmakers benefit from cheaper coal can therefore be misleading. Which coal, which process and which proportion of the cost base? [1]

The same caution applies to environmental claims. An electric furnace is not evidence that the entire product chain has no emissions. The metallic input, electricity, fuel and upstream activities still need to be considered.

For business analysis, I would reduce the engineering to a practical sheet: input basket, energy requirement, saleable yield, product forms, operating constraints and the spending needed to maintain the asset. That is more useful than ranking companies by a process label alone.

3. Does higher iron content always mean a better mine?

Iron content is important, but it is only part of the product specification. The buyer also cares about impurities, moisture, physical form, consistency, recovery and the process in which the material will be used.

Ore, concentrate and pellets are different products. Comparing their selling prices without adjusting for preparation, yield, freight and customer requirements can create a false ranking of mine quality.

GPIL's FY2025 report separately describes mining, beneficiation and pellet operations. Beneficiation can improve the usable input, but it has capital, operating and recovery requirements. A higher-grade output does not appear without a cost or a material balance. [6]

I would compare delivered economics for a defined product. What comes out of the ground, how much becomes saleable, what processing is required and what reaches the customer after freight? A price premium is attractive only when the cost of earning it leaves an adequate return.

4. What actually changes a miner's earnings?

The first bridge is saleable volume multiplied by realised price. Then deduct extraction, processing, logistics, statutory and contractual payments, and the other costs relevant to the business.

Volume can improve through existing equipment working harder, new approvals, better recovery or expansion. Those are different events. Permission to produce more is not production, and production is not a completed sale or collection.

A low-cost mine can benefit sharply when selling prices rise, but the reverse also matters. A business with little control over the selling price needs a cost and balance-sheet advantage that can survive weaker conditions.

Do not describe mining returns as infinite because an approval appears to unlock more output with limited machinery. The mineral right, development spending, environmental obligations, working capital and opportunity cost of the resource all remain economically relevant. Low incremental equipment spending is a narrow observation, not a complete return calculation.

5. Why must the lease and auction terms be read?

A valuable deposit and the right to extract it are different things. Lease duration, permitted operations, payment obligations, development requirements and environmental conditions can determine how much of the apparent mineral value reaches shareholders.

The Ministry of Mines booklet describes an auction framework in which the bid is a percentage of the value of mineral despatched. It explains a reference based on despatched quantity and the grade-wise, state-wise sale price published by the Indian Bureau of Mines. This should not be confused with a simple percentage of accounting profit. [7]

For an original illustration, suppose a defined payment is 50% of a benchmark value of ₹4,000 per tonne. That payment would be ₹2,000 per tonne under those assumptions. It says nothing by itself about the mine's actual selling price, extraction cost or all the other charges. It is not an estimate of any company's obligation.

I would build the actual payment bridge from the applicable documents rather than combine percentages from unrelated examples. Also separate physical reserve life from legal tenure. Enough ore for many years does not automatically extend a right that expires sooner.

6. How is a converter different from a commodity producer?

A converter buys an input and sells a product with additional processing, specification or service. Its earnings can depend more on the retained conversion spread than on the gross value of metal passing through its accounts.

Consider a hypothetical converter selling 100 units. Initially, each sells for 100 and carries a metal cost of 70, leaving 30 per unit before conversion costs. If both selling price and metal cost fall by 20, revenue falls from 10,000 to 8,000 while that simplified spread remains 3,000.

This is not a claim that all converters have automatic pass-through. The actual formula, purchase timing, product mix and customer bargaining power decide whether the spread survives.

The producer-versus-converter distinction is a research question. It should be demonstrated in contracts and through-cycle results, not assigned because a company describes itself as value-added.

7. Can product qualification create an advantage?

A rope used in a crane or a casting designed for industrial equipment is more than an unspecified weight of steel. Performance, testing, consistency, delivery and the cost of failure can matter to the customer.

Usha Martin's FY2025 report describes application-specific rope products, development work, testing, certification and customer service. These are useful things to investigate when judging differentiation. The company's own account is evidence of its activities and strategy, not independent proof that competitors cannot qualify. [2]

My questions would be practical. How long does approval take? Does the approval attach to a product, process or plant? How often are suppliers retested? Does the customer have a second source? Who pays if the product fails?

Then examine the commercial result. Better qualification should eventually show up in retained business, mix, pricing or returns. A long list of certificates without adequate cash generation is not enough.

Large upstream steelmakers can also move downstream. Standard products and genuinely customer-specific products may face different entry conditions, but small market size alone is not permanent protection from competition.

8. Why can a good casting or rope business still be cyclical?

The product can be difficult to replace while the customer's order volume remains variable. A supplier to mining equipment, construction machinery, ports or offshore projects can face fewer orders when customers slow investment or reduce inventory.

Steelcast's FY2025 report discusses the effects of lower production, underutilisation and subsequent operating improvement. That is a useful reminder to distinguish product capability from demand. A qualified supplier does not control the customer's capital budget. [5]

Fixed costs can amplify the effect. Lower tonnes spread factory overhead across fewer units. Labour, maintenance, development and customer-support spending may not fall in proportion to sales.

I would look at volume and profit per tonne together, then reconcile product mix and utilisation. A stable percentage margin can hide lower absolute profit. An improving unit margin can also mask weak demand if the company is selling fewer, more expensive products.

Customer diversification helps only if the customers' demand drivers differ. Ten customers all tied to the same equipment cycle may provide less protection than the count suggests.

9. What can distort the reported conversion margin?

Input-price movements, pass-through lags, foreign exchange, energy costs, inventory effects, product mix and assistance income can all change a reported margin. Separate these before calling an improvement structural.

Bharat Wire Ropes' FY2025 report identifies competition, raw-material availability and price fluctuations among its risks. A value-added product does not make the purchase cost of wire rod or the timing of customer repricing irrelevant. [3]

10. Is stainless steel one premium product?

No. Different grades are designed for different requirements. Composition, corrosion performance, strength, formability, welding and the customer's application all matter. A series number should not be treated as a universal ranking of product quality or economic value.

Jindal Stainless' FY2025 report describes products across 200, 300 and 400 series and a range of end markets, together with investments across the chain. That is a reason to study its mix, not to assume every tonne earns the same spread or requires the same input basket. [4]

Nickel, chromium, scrap and other input exposures need to be matched to the actual grade and process. Lower raw-material prices may help purchasing but can also affect selling prices and inventory values. The net result depends on timing and commercial terms.

I would also separate a raw-material supply arrangement, a smelter interest and ownership of a mine. They confer different rights, obligations and risks. The phrase backward integration is not a sufficient description of what shareholders actually own.

11. Does captive supply automatically create value?

Captive ore or power can improve reliability and reduce exposure to an external supplier. But internal supply has an economic cost even when no outside invoice is raised. Capital, maintenance, operating costs and foregone outside sales still matter.

For an integrated group, avoid counting the same saving twice. A mine selling internally and a downstream plant buying internally do not create two separate group profits simply because an internal transfer price is recorded.

GPIL's FY2025 report separates mining, beneficiation, pellets, sponge iron, steel melting and downstream operations. Their capacities are different stages of a chain. They cannot simply be added into a single meaningful production total. [6]

The useful questions are the material balance, external sales and purchases, utilisation at each stage and the bottleneck. Integration can solve one constraint while creating a larger funding or execution commitment elsewhere.

12. How should an expansion be tested?

Start with existing capacity, incremental capacity and final capacity in separate columns. Then distinguish announced, approved, under construction, commissioned and commercially utilised capacity.

A plan to add two million tonnes to a base of three million means five million after expansion, not two million in total and not eight million by counting both the addition and final total. The same care applies to monthly versus annual measures.

GPIL's historical report explicitly separates current and post-expansion capacities and expected commissioning periods. Those dates describe expectations as of the report, not confirmation of present operating status. [6]

I would attach the full cash requirement, approvals, ramp-up, product qualification and working capital to each phase. New capacity does not earn the mature plant's utilisation on day one.

Finally, test incremental returns at conservative prices and demand. A company that made money from a favourable mine cost may be entering a much larger steelmaking project with different capital intensity. The earlier advantage does not prove the next investment will earn the same return.

13. What would make the investment thesis testable?

I would want one operating bridge for each economic layer: saleable volume, realisation, input and conversion cost, cash margin, capital needed and cash collected. Use consistent units, periods and entity scopes.

For a miner, track rights, saleable grade, recovery, statutory payments, cost and evacuation. For a producer, add process and input mix, energy, commodity spreads and debt. For a converter, focus on qualification, volume, retained spread, customer concentration and working capital.

The downside case should include lower selling prices, weaker customer orders, slower pass-through, project delays and financing pressure. Peak-cycle earnings should not become a permanent base merely because the current multiple looks low.

The distinction I would keep returning to is simple: commodity exposure, conversion capability and capital allocation are separate questions. A good answer to one does not settle the other two.
