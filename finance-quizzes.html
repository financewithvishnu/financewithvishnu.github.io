Chapter 1. Money, Banking and the Financial System

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. Whose IOU is the balance in your savings account?

Answer and explanation

Answer: The bank's IOU to you

A bank deposit is the bank's liability to its depositor. Chapter 1 contrasts this with cash, which is described as the state's IOU.

Review Chapter 1, Section 1.1

2. In the chapter's simplified money-multiplier example, what happens when the reserve ratio rises from 10% to 20%?

Answer and explanation

Answer: The illustrated deposit ceiling falls from ₹10 lakh to ₹5 lakh

For the same ₹1 lakh starting deposit, the simple ceiling is ₹1 lakh divided by the reserve ratio. It falls from ₹10 lakh to ₹5 lakh. This is an illustration, not a prediction of actual lending.

Review Chapter 1, Section 1.2

3. You buy a company's bond directly through a market. Who bears that issuer's default risk?

Answer and explanation

Answer: The bond investor bears the issuer's credit risk

The chapter distinguishes an intermediary lending in its own name from an investor owning a borrower's security. Listing and the ability to trade do not remove the issuer's default risk.

Chapter 2. Interest Rates, Inflation and the Time Value of Money

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. What does compound interest do that simple interest does not?

Answer and explanation

Answer: It earns interest on accumulated interest as well as principal

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. With compounding, earlier interest also earns interest, so the gap grows with time, assuming returns are reinvested.

Review Chapter 2, Section 2.1

2. Using the Rule of 72, what annual inflation rate roughly doubles prices in 12 years?

Answer and explanation

Answer: 6%

The Rule of 72 gives an approximate doubling time: years ≈ 72 ÷ annual percentage rate. Therefore 72 ÷ 12 ≈ 6% annual inflation. This is an approximation, not a forecast.

Review Chapter 2, Section 2.3

3. An FD pays 7%, inflation is 6%, and tax is ignored. What is the approximate real return?

Answer and explanation

Answer: About 1%

The chapter's approximation is nominal return minus inflation: 7% - 6% = 1%. The exact calculation, 1.07/1.06 - 1, is about 0.94%. Tax would reduce it further.

Review Chapter 2, Section 2.4

Chapter 3. Risk and Return: The Grammar of Investing

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. A bond pays income equal to 9% of its starting value and its price falls 3% over the year. Ignoring costs, what is its total return?

Answer and explanation

Answer: About 6%

Total return includes both income and the price change. On the stated starting-value basis, 9% + (-3%) = 6%, before costs and tax.

Review Chapter 3, Section 3.1

2. Which set separates the three parts of personal risk tolerance used in the chapter?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Ability, willingness and need

Ability concerns financial capacity and horizon; willingness concerns tolerance for losses and swings; need concerns the return required by goals. A claim of high risk appetite does not settle all three.

Review Chapter 3, Section 3.3

3. You own HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, SBI and Axis Bank. Which is the best description of the diversification?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Several holdings can still share one banking-sector exposure

Different company names do not guarantee different risks. These holdings share banking-sector drivers. The chapter stresses genuinely different exposures and warns that correlations can rise in a panic.

Review Chapter 3, Section 3.4

Chapter 4. The Investor's Toolkit: Accounts and Market Plumbing

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. Which account holds your shares in electronic form?

Answer and explanation

Answer: The demat account with a depository

The demat account holds electronic securities through NSDL or CDSL. The trading account is used to place orders; the bank account holds money.

Review Chapter 4, Section 4.1

2. Which institution becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer after an exchange trade is matched?

Answer and explanation

Answer: The clearing corporation

The clearing corporation steps between the matched parties as central counterparty. The exchange matches orders; the depository records securities ownership. This role does not protect an investment from price losses.

Review Chapter 4, Section 4.2

3. What does T+1 settlement mean?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Settlement one working day after the trade date

T is the trade date. T+1 means the next working day, subject to the applicable settlement calendar, rather than simply the next calendar day.

Review Chapter 4, Section 4.3

Chapter 5. Budgeting, Emergency Funds and Financial Goals

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. Which habit addresses the problem of saving only what is left after spending?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Decide the saving first and automate it when income arrives

The chapter starts with income minus expenses. Its practical habit is to decide savings first and automate the transfer on income day, while treating percentage budgets as guides rather than laws.

Review Chapter 5, Section 5.1

2. What is the main job of an emergency fund?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Provide accessible money so a crisis does not force distressed selling or expensive borrowing

An emergency can arrive without warning. The chapter puts liquidity and certainty ahead of return for this reserve, so market risk or a lock-in can defeat its purpose.

Review Chapter 5, Section 5.2

3. Assets are ₹17 lakh and outstanding liabilities are ₹5 lakh. What is net worth?

Answer and explanation

Answer: ₹12 lakh

Net worth is assets minus liabilities: ₹17 lakh - ₹5 lakh = ₹12 lakh. One snapshot alone cannot tell you whether the person's net worth is rising or falling.

Review Chapter 5, Section 5.5

Chapter 6. Insurance as Risk Transfer

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. What is the central job of insurance?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Transfer a specified financial risk in exchange for a premium

Insurance exchanges a small, certain premium for cover against a large, uncertain loss within the policy terms. Investing has a different purpose, so a bundled product should be unbundled before comparing it.

Review Chapter 6, Section 6.1

2. Why can a plain term life plan be cheaper than a plan with a survival payout?

Answer and explanation

Answer: It charges only for protection and associated costs, without a bundled survival benefit

A plain term plan pays the specified death benefit during the term, subject to its terms, but no survival benefit. The absence of that savings component helps keep pure protection cheaper.

Review Chapter 6, Section 6.2

3. A health policy's room-rent limit is ₹5,000 a day, but the chosen room costs ₹10,000. In the chapter's example, what can a proportionate-deduction clause affect?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Other linked hospital charges as well as the room charge

In the chapter's worked example, taking a room at twice the allowed rate also reduces linked charges proportionately. Actual deductions and exceptions depend on the policy wording, so this is not a universal claim-settlement rule.

Review Chapter 6, Section 6.3

Chapter 7. Loans, Credit Scores and Borrowing Wisely

Read the chapter · Original recall questions

1. Why does a long home loan's principal often decline slowly in its early years?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Interest on the large outstanding balance takes a larger part of the EMI

Each EMI covers interest on the remaining balance and a principal repayment. Early on, a large balance means a larger interest component. The split shifts as principal is repaid.

Review Chapter 7, Section 7.2

2. Which behaviour is most consistent with building a sound credit history?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Paying EMIs and card bills on time while keeping utilisation low

The chapter stresses on-time payments, low utilisation and a long, clean history. Multiple hurried applications and missed payments can hurt rather than help. No single behaviour guarantees a particular score.

Review Chapter 7, Section 7.3

3. Which action turns a credit card from a payment tool into carried borrowing?

Answer and explanation

Answer: Paying only the minimum due and carrying the rest

Paying only the minimum leaves a revolving balance and can trigger costly interest under the card's terms. Paying in full by the due date keeps the payment-tool distinction described in the chapter.

Review Chapter 7, Section 7.5
