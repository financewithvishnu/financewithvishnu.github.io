Gold lenders: collateral is the start of safety, not the end

By Finance With Vishnu · Source period: March 2021

Why would a borrower pay a specialist more than a bank for a loan secured by the same jewellery? The answer can be speed, proximity, familiar staff, flexible access and confidence that the item will be returned safely.

That service bundle can support a valuable lending business. It does not make the loan risk-free. The gold can fall in price, interest can consume the cushion, appraisal can be wrong and funding can mature before recovery arrives.

I would study a gold lender as three connected operations: a customer-service business, a physical-collateral business and a leveraged financial business. Strength in one cannot excuse weakness in the others.

1. What does the customer actually buy?

A borrower pledges eligible gold, receives a loan and must repay under agreed terms to recover the item. The customer is buying liquidity without an outright sale, along with appraisal, custody and redemption service.

A nearby specialist can reduce travel, paperwork and waiting. Those benefits may matter greatly for a small urgent transaction. But willingness to pay for convenience has limits, especially when a short loan is repeatedly renewed.

The World Gold Council's dated study of gold in India's financial system describes both jewellery's household role and the growth of organised gold lending. Its market estimates are historical and scope-dependent. They support studying the service model, not a guaranteed market share or growth forecast. [1]

Emotional attachment can encourage repayment. It does not establish repayment capacity. A family can desperately want its jewellery back and still lose income or face a medical emergency.

Fair pricing, clear charges and respectful collection are therefore part of franchise quality. A durable advantage should not rely on distress, confusion or customers being unable to leave.

2. How does the lender earn money?

The basic bridge is interest and eligible fees less funding cost, operating expenses, credit and operational losses, and tax. Lending also requires equity and liquidity. A high customer yield is not the final return to shareholders.

Specialists may fund through banks, bonds and other permitted sources, alongside equity. They do not possess a bank's ordinary demand-deposit franchise. RBI's NBFC FAQ distinguishes public funds from public deposits and demand deposits. Bank borrowing and debentures can be public funds; that is not the same as permission to accept every kind of deposit. [2]

Calculate asset yield and funding cost on consistent average balances. Then distinguish spread from net interest margin, operating profit, return on assets and return on equity. Their denominators differ.

Higher leverage can raise ROE without improving the underlying lending economics. A lender paying a lower funding rate may still earn less if it prices aggressively, runs expensive branches or suffers more losses.

Growth needs cash to fund new loans and enough capital to support them. Accounting profit is not automatically a pool available for dividends or unrelated expansion.

3. What is the real collateral value?

A jewellery invoice can include making charges, stones, tax and other elements that are not recoverable metal value. Eligible weight, actual purity and the prescribed valuation basis matter more than the original purchase price.

RBI's 2025 Gold and Silver Collateral Directions distinguish intrinsic metal value from other cost elements and specify assaying and documentation requirements. These are later regulatory references, not rules assumed available in FY2021 or a determination for a particular loan. [3]

Suppose an item originally cost 120, including 20 of non-metal costs, while its assessed eligible metal value is 100. Advancing an illustrative 75% of that metal value gives a loan of 75, not 90. The lender cannot assume an auction will recover the customer's entire original bill.

Borrower ownership, authenticity, purity, weight, seals, records and physical possession all need controls. A gold-price cushion does not protect against an item that was never worth the assessed amount.

Professional custody is valuable only if identification, storage, transit, insurance, audit and release processes work. Insurance scope and recovery timing need checking rather than treating every loss as fully reimbursed.

4. How quickly can the cushion disappear?

Look at total recoverable dues and net sale proceeds, not just original principal against today's headline gold price. Unpaid interest, costs and delays can erode coverage even without a large price shock.

The rate, tenor and advance are teaching assumptions. Real loan terms and regulatory calculations can differ. The 2025 directions, for example, define LTV for bullet-repayment loans using the total amount repayable at maturity. A principal-only teaching ratio should not be passed off as the complete regulatory calculation. [3]

A lower advance helps, but price gaps, fake collateral, missing items and delayed enforcement remain. Falling prices do not automatically make a lender safer merely because it might later request additional collateral.

5. What does a shorter tenor really achieve?

An earlier maturity gives the lender an opportunity to collect, reassess collateral and renew exposure on an appropriate basis. It does not prove that cash has actually returned.

A customer who repeatedly rolls a facility can remain indebted much longer than the contractual tenor suggests. Read actual repayments, interest collections, top-ups, renewals and overdue migration. A new contract date cannot by itself repair insufficient recovery coverage.

Keep principal repayment and interest timing separate. Some structures collect interest periodically and principal at maturity; others require both at maturity. Under the referenced 2025 directions, the defined bullet-repayment category means both principal and interest due at maturity. Use the applicable definition rather than casual market shorthand. [3]

Specialist flexibility can help a borrower match a temporary cash need. It can also disguise dependence on recurring refinancing. The investor needs evidence that customers can redeem, not merely that the lender can renew.

Prompt recovery can protect capital, but it must follow fair process. Customer harm and lost trust can weaken future economics even where immediate credit loss is small.

6. Is AUM growth really customer growth?

Gold-loan assets under management are influenced by pledged eligible weight, assessed value per unit and effective loan utilisation. The relationship is approximate because vintages, revaluations and reporting definitions differ.

A borrower needing a fixed cash amount may pledge less weight when prices rise. Another may borrow more against the same item. Neither behaviour is a universal law.

Muthoot Finance's FY2025 report separates gold-loan, standalone and consolidated AUM. Manappuram's FY2025 report separately discusses gold holdings, gold lending and other businesses. Those are useful perimeter checks before interpreting any growth headline. [4, 5]

A large national jewellery stock is potential collateral, not an immediately addressable loan book. Willingness to pledge, eligibility, borrowing need and competition constrain conversion.

7. What do auctions and NPAs tell us?

Default probability and ultimate loss severity are different. A loan can become non-performing and later recover through repayment or sale of collateral. Low net write-offs do not prove there were no overdue borrowers or economic costs.

Compare gross NPA, net NPA, provisions, interest reversal, auction volume, recovery shortfall and final write-offs on matched definitions. Interest reversal removes recognised income from the accounts on the applicable basis; it is not automatically a contractual waiver of everything the borrower owes.

Absolute auction value needs the AUM and vintage denominator. Fewer auctions might reflect better collections, different product design or delayed enforcement. The number alone cannot identify the cause.

The 2025 directions address borrower notice, auction transparency, adjustment of dues and return of surplus. Surplus proceeds are not unrestricted additional lender profit. This article does not reproduce an old class description as today's universal auction deadline. [3]

Measure time to release jewellery after discharge and complaints alongside recovery performance. The product's trust proposition depends on both safe lending and dependable redemption.

8. Can a short secured book still have liquidity risk?

Yes. Contractual maturity is not the same as cash availability. A loan due before the lender's borrowing matures may still be unpaid when creditors demand cash.

Gold is generally easier to value and sell than a specialised factory, but appraisal disputes, auction process, price discounts and operational capacity still take time. Refinancing can become scarce just when customers struggle and collateral prices move.

Read the maturity ladder, unencumbered liquidity, committed lines, lender concentration, security, covenants and currency or hedging exposure. Several bank relationships are not independent protection if all banks reduce sector exposure together.

A lower-cost commercial-paper programme can improve near-term spread while increasing refinancing dependence. Bond-market access and a favourable rating are information to assess, not certificates of safety.

I would stress both slower collections and reduced new borrowing capacity. A model that assumes continued loan growth funds every liability maturity has not demonstrated resilience.

9. Does digital lending remove the physical operation?

No. Online redraw can be convenient after gold is lodged, verified and held under a valid facility. The app does not make the loan unsecured or remove the need for custody and limits.

Manappuram's FY2025 report discusses online gold lending alongside physical branches and gold in custody. That combination is the right operational picture. A digital-channel percentage still needs its denominator: customers, disbursals, transactions and AUM are different measures. [5]

Doorstep collection adds identity, transit, appraisal and insurance questions. A fintech using a bank partner may reduce its own branch requirement, while introducing partner dependence and a revenue-sharing arrangement.

Identify the lender, custodian, distributor and risk-bearer before comparing economics. An app's growth can benefit several parties differently.

Technology can lower incumbent servicing costs and also reduce entry barriers for competitors. Test cost per transaction, retention, acquisition cost, fraud and return on new branches instead of assuming every digital initiative strengthens the moat.

10. When does diversification weaken the gold franchise?

Microfinance, housing, vehicles and unsecured lending are not simply more gold loans under different names. They have different borrowers, credit evidence, collateral, tenor, collection and funding needs.

Diversification can broaden earnings and create useful distribution links. It can also consume capital earned by the core business. Manappuram's FY2025 report discusses significant stress at its microfinance subsidiary during that year. Muthoot's FY2025 report distinguishes subsidiary contributions to group AUM from contributions to profit. These later disclosures illustrate why asset mix is not profit or risk mix. [4, 5]

Parent support can be required before future earnings arrive. A plan to cap geographic exposure is not evidence the actual portfolio already meets it. Provisioning absorbs recognised loss; it does not prevent borrowers defaulting.

Use separate segment scorecards and a group support map. Do not grant a rerating simply because the group owns more types of financial business.

11. Can controls matter more than the gold price?

Yes. Appraisal errors, fraud, custody failures, incomplete records and conduct problems can damage a lender even in a favourable commodity environment.

IIFL Finance's July 2025 exchange filing describes a temporary 2024 gold-loan embargo, later resumption and corrective actions. This is a dated company disclosure, not a claim about restrictions today. It demonstrates why operational and regulatory controls belong in the business model rather than in a footnote. [6]

RBI's dated 2025 directions likewise address assaying, records, custody, documentation, release and auction conduct. Lending is not made safe solely by setting an origination ratio. [3]

Ask whether cost reductions preserve independent checks, insurance scope, security and audit. A lower guard or branch expense is not an improvement if uninsured losses or weak controls offset it.

For a current borrower decision, inspect the exact lender, agreement, key facts, charges and applicable rules. A historical convenience claim is not a promise of instant approval, free storage or unconditional 24-hour access.

12. How should earnings and book value be connected?

They are not rival valuation systems. On matching bases, price/book equals price/earnings multiplied by earnings/book equity. Published ROE often uses average equity while price/book uses closing equity, so align denominators before applying the identity.

High sustainable returns can support a higher book multiple. Under-provisioning, unusual gold prices, cheap temporary funding or excessive leverage can also make reported returns look high.

Do not apply a mature gold-lending multiple to every subsidiary. Value the appropriate attributable economics and subtract parent obligations without double-counting debt or minority claims.

A proposed demerger or improved market perception is a possible scenario, not guaranteed value creation. The original source-era rerating opinions are not treated here as completed catalysts or present targets.

13. What would earn confidence in the franchise?

Evidence of durable service advantages, careful appraisal, safe custody, realistic collateral coverage, transparent collections and funding that survives a difficult period.

Build a growth bridge from customers, weight, price and utilisation. Build a profit bridge from yield through funding, operating costs and losses. Then test capital, liquidity and subsidiaries.

Stress a sharp gold-price fall, slower redemption, recovery delays, rising funding cost, a custody incident and correlated non-gold losses. The same event can affect cash, profit and trust at once.

The strongest gold lender is not the one described as having no credit risk. It is the one that can explain, measure and fund the risks that remain after taking the gold.
