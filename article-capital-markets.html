Capital markets: who earns when investors save, trade and raise money?

By Finance With Vishnu · Source period: approximately May-June 2025 · Sector study

An investor buys a mutual fund, trades a share or participates in a new issue. Behind that decision sit several businesses. Some manage investments. Some bring customers to products. Others process transactions, maintain records or provide the market in which trading takes place.

I would not begin by asking which company benefits from more investors. That is too broad. I would ask what each business charges for, what determines the fee and which risks it must carry to earn it.

A growing financial system can support many businesses, but it does not remove market cycles. Assets, transactions, fees and costs can move in different directions. Understanding those differences is the starting point.

1. What does the capital-market value chain do?

Different businesses connect people with capital, investment products and transaction services. Their roles overlap in some groups, but the economic activity should still be separated.

An asset manager makes investment decisions within a product's mandate. A distributor helps products reach clients. A broker provides access to trading. Exchanges, clearing services, depositories and registrars perform different parts of market operation and record keeping.

These are not interchangeable licences or sources of income. I would identify the legal entity, service and revenue line before combining businesses into a group-level story.

The same investor activity can create revenue for several providers. Adding all their client assets together would not measure a new pool of investor wealth.

2. Which number actually creates revenue?

Assets under management, assets under custody, client assets, folios, accounts and trading turnover describe different things. None is automatically the provider's own revenue or capital.

For an asset-linked fee, a useful starting model is average fee-paying assets multiplied by the effective fee yield. For activity-linked income, start with billable activity multiplied by the amount retained per billable unit.

A periodic issuer fee follows another charging basis. A completed investment-banking assignment may produce a fee that does not repeat the next quarter. A lending operation earns a spread but also consumes funding and carries credit risk.

I would therefore build the revenue bridge one line at a time. Growth in a headline operating metric is only useful after establishing how it is billed.

Definitions also matter across peers. One business may report gross revenue before distribution payouts while another emphasises net revenue. A seemingly higher margin can partly reflect a different denominator rather than a better underlying business.

3. Why can AUM grow faster than asset-management fees?

An asset manager's revenue depends on both the amount and composition of assets. Different products can earn different fees, and a change in mix can offset part of the benefit of a larger asset base.

HDFC AMC's FY2025 annual report explains that its investment-management fees vary across equity and debt schemes, fund composition and fund size. It also distinguishes mutual-fund fees from other services. The important lesson is the relationship, not the assumption of one permanent yield. [1]

Consider a hypothetical manager with average fee-paying assets of ₹10,000 crore and an annual yield of 50 basis points. One basis point is 0.01 percentage point, so revenue is ₹50 crore. If assets rise 20% to ₹12,000 crore while yield falls to 40 basis points, revenue is ₹48 crore.

AUM grew, yet revenue fell 4%. This is an original illustration, not a stated fee schedule. It shows why asset growth alone cannot establish earnings growth.

I would inspect average assets, product mix, fee-paying versus non-fee-paying assets and the net amount retained. The total expense ratio charged to a fund is not automatically identical to the asset manager's retained revenue.

4. Is AUM growth the same as fresh investor money?

No. Asset values can rise because markets rise, because investors add money or because both happen. Redemptions and distributions can reduce the asset base.

A simplified bridge is opening assets plus net flows plus investment-value changes, with relevant adjustments. The precise bridge depends on the product and reporting definition.

Suppose opening assets are ₹10,000 crore. Net new money adds ₹1,000 crore and market-value changes add ₹2,000 crore. Closing assets are ₹13,000 crore. Calling the entire 30% increase new customer money would misdescribe the business.

Recurring contributions do not guarantee that total net flows stay positive. Other investors can redeem, and market declines can reduce the fee base. I would separate customer acquisition and retention from the benefit of rising prices.

5. What makes wealth-management revenue recurring?

Wealth management can include advice, distribution, managed products, transactions and lending. A client relationship does not make every rupee of revenue recurring.

Nuvama's FY2025 presentation separates recurring and transactional revenue measures, fee-earning assets and net new money. It also separates wealth, asset management, asset services and capital-market activities. That is a useful reminder to inspect the mix beneath the group label. [2]

A fee earned periodically on retained assets differs from an upfront product commission or a fee from a single transaction. Even recurring asset-linked income can fall when assets decline, clients leave or pricing changes.

People are part of the cost structure. Relationship managers, service teams and product specialists must be paid and retained. Hiring can raise costs before new teams produce enough revenue to cover them.

I would track recurring assets, net new money, yield, client concentration and cost-to-income using consistent definitions. A higher recurring share can improve visibility, but it does not remove the need to examine client outcomes, incentives and the cost of servicing those relationships.

6. Why are brokerage customers not the same as brokerage earnings?

A registered customer may be inactive, trade infrequently or use services with different economics. Customer additions should be connected to activity, retention and the net revenue earned after relevant payouts.

The broker may earn from trading, distribution, financing and other services. The mix determines how changes in trading activity affect the whole business.

ICRA's August 2025 review of Angel One discusses pressure following changes to the index-derivatives framework, lower order activity and marketing costs. It also describes capital-market lending and diversification efforts. This is a dated later cross-check, not a claim about the company's current performance. [3]

I would compare acquisition cost with the profit generated by a retained customer over time. A cheap account opening is not necessarily cheap acquisition if the customer never becomes economically active.

The reverse also matters. A strong quarter of trading income can make acquisition spending appear unusually productive. Test the relationship under more ordinary activity before assuming that the same payback period will persist.

7. Why must exchange volumes be measured carefully?

A trade count, contract count, cash-market turnover, options premium turnover and notional turnover are not interchangeable measures. Each answers a different question.

BSE's FY2025 results release reports multiple operating measures and separate businesses, including equity derivatives and its mutual-fund transaction platform. It also distinguishes operating revenue from total revenue. A single volume headline cannot explain every revenue line. [4]

I would identify the relevant billable base, product mix and effective fee retained. Market share can be valuable when it brings activity that generates an adequate return. It is less informative without the pricing and cost context.

An exchange can benefit when buyers and sellers find liquidity in the same place. But competition, product design, technology reliability and regulation still influence where activity goes.

Higher traffic also requires dependable systems and risk controls. I would not assume that infrastructure spending stops once the platform reaches scale. Service failure can damage the very trust and activity that support the business.

8. How is a depository different from a broker or registrar?

A depository maintains securities in electronic form through its operating framework and participants. It is not the same business as a broker providing trading access or a mutual-fund registrar processing fund-investor records.

CDSL's May 2025 earnings-call transcript discusses transaction charges, annual issuer charges and other activities separately. It also explains that issuer billing involves a defined folio basis, which is not simply the number of unique people investing. [5]

I would not treat every demat account as a distinct investor, or every account as equally active and equally profitable. The connection between an operating count and revenue depends on the particular service and charging terms.

The same applies to different group activities. Depository services, identity-related services and other subsidiaries may have different customers, competition and costs.

The research task is to understand each material revenue stream and its exposure to transaction activity, issuer participation and pricing. A familiar infrastructure label should not replace that work.

9. Why can record keeping be a valuable business?

Investors need accurate holdings, transaction processing and service. Funds need dependable records and operational support. A provider that performs this work at scale can become deeply integrated into its clients' processes.

CAMS's FY2025 management discussion describes its mutual-fund registrar and transfer-agency activity, investor servicing and other financial-infrastructure businesses. It also identifies customer concentration, technology, data protection and fee pressure as risks. [6]

That combination is important. Operational importance can support customer retention, but it does not create unlimited pricing power. Clients still negotiate, and regulation and competition can affect what they are willing or able to pay.

I would look for the cost of reliable processing, technology investment, service quality and the concentration of revenue in major clients. High margins do not mean that the service can be provided without continued investment.

Nor would I turn today's concentrated market structure into a promise that no new competitor can ever emerge. The exact service category and its providers must be checked rather than inferred from a broad industry label.

10. What changes when a business helps raise capital?

Investment banking can include work on capital raising and corporate transactions. The fee depends on the assignment and its terms, not simply on the number of potential deals discussed.

Nuvama's FY2025 presentation describes institutional equities and investment banking within its capital-market business, alongside a wider group offering. That mix should be separated from recurring wealth income when judging the group's earnings cycle. [2]

A deal pipeline is not the same as completed, recognised and collected revenue. Market conditions, issuer decisions and execution can affect whether an assignment reaches the fee-generating stage.

I would examine completed activity over several years, revenue concentration, the people required to win and deliver mandates, and any risks retained under specific contracts. A strong issuance year should not automatically become the permanent base case.

This is also why comparisons across groups need care. Two businesses called financial-services companies can have very different proportions of transactional fees, asset-linked fees, lending income and investment gains.

11. How does operating leverage affect the cycle?

Technology, compliance, offices and core staff create costs that do not fall immediately when revenue weakens. Other costs vary more directly with business activity.

In a hypothetical provider, revenue is ₹100 crore, variable costs are 20% of revenue and fixed costs are ₹50 crore. Operating surplus before depreciation, financing, tax and other excluded items is ₹30 crore.

If revenue falls 20% to ₹80 crore with the same cost assumptions, variable costs become ₹16 crore and surplus falls to ₹14 crore. Revenue fell one fifth, while surplus fell about 53.3%.

Actual costs can adjust, and business lines differ. The point is to test a plausible downturn rather than describe a high-margin quarter as the business's permanent earning power.

12. Where do capital, regulation and investor outcomes enter?

Not every capital-market business is equally asset-light. ICRA's Angel One review describes funding and capital needs as its margin-trading finance business grows, along with credit and market risks. A fee business and a lending book should not be analysed using the same assumptions. [3]

I would also separate client assets and restricted balances from the company's freely available cash. A large balance associated with customer activity does not automatically belong to shareholders for distribution.

Regulation can change the economics of a service. Changes to product rules, fees or operating requirements may alter activity or costs. This study's historical examples are not a guide to today's trading permissions.

Investor outcomes matter as well. SEBI's September 2024 study reported that 93% of individual equity futures-and-options traders incurred losses over FY2022-FY2024. That is a historical finding for a specified population and period, not a probability forecast for every investment activity. [7]

A provider earning from activity does not establish that more activity benefits the customer. Sustainable business quality requires attention to conduct, suitability, controls and trust, alongside revenue growth.

13. What belongs in the research file?

I would gain confidence when a business retains customers, earns repeat fees for useful work and invests enough to deliver reliably. I would be more cautious when the case rests on a record trading quarter, undifferentiated client-asset totals or an acquisition-led change in business mix that has not yet proved its economics.

Financial participation is the opportunity. The business question is who earns from it, how much they retain and what they must put at risk to do so.
