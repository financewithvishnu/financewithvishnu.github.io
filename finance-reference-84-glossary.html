Chapter 84. Glossary and Abbreviations

Chapter 84 of 85 · reference · look things up as they come

Every term this book relies on, in plain language, each defined the way the book uses it. When a chapter sends a word past too fast, it lands here. One line each, no hedging.

84.1A-Z glossary of terms used in the book

Alphareturn beyond what market exposure (beta) explains; skill, luck, or fees eaten in reverse.
Amortisationpaying a loan down in scheduled slices; also spreading an intangible's cost over its life.
Arbitragesimultaneous buy and sell of the same economic thing in two places for a near-riskless gap.
Ask / bidthe price sellers want / buyers offer; the gap between them is the spread.
Asset allocationthe split of a portfolio across equity, debt, gold, cash; the decision that drives most outcomes.
AT1 bondperpetual bank capital that can be written down before equity; Yes Bank 2020 taught the fine print.
Basis pointone hundredth of a percent; 25 bps is 0.25%.
Bear / bull marketa fall of 20%+ from the peak / a sustained rise; mood nouns with a measuring tape.
Betaa stock's sensitivity to the market: 1.2 swings about 20% more than the index.
Bonda tradable loan: face value, coupon, maturity, yield; price falls when yields rise.
Book valueassets minus liabilities per the balance sheet; per share it anchors P/B.
CAGRcompound annual growth rate; correct for lump sums, wrong for money with flows (use XIRR).
Circuit breakeran automatic trading halt at set index or stock moves; the market's seatbelt.
Compoundingreturns earning returns; the eighth wonder and the reason for Chapter 1.
Coupona bond's stated interest payment, set on face value.
Credit ratingan agency's opinion of default risk, AAA down to D; opinions that trail reality, per IL&FS.
Current ratiocurrent assets over current liabilities; short-term solvency in one number.
Debenturea corporate bond; secured or not, rated or not, read the trust deed.
Dematdematerialised; shares held electronically at NSDL/CDSL instead of paper certificates.
Derivativescontracts priced off something else: futures, options, swaps; tools and traps of Part VII.
Diversificationowning assets that do not fall together; the only free lunch, priced in correlation.
Dividend yieldannual dividend over price; income in percentage form.
Duration (bond)weighted average time to a bond's cash flows; modified version predicts price moves per 1% yield change.
EBIT / EBITDAearnings before interest and tax; EBITDA adds back depreciation and amortisation, and flatters accordingly.
EPSearnings per share; profit attributable to each share, the E in P/E.
ETFa fund traded like a stock, usually tracking an index; low cost, intraday liquidity.
Face valuea share's nominal capital value or a bond's redemption value; mostly ceremonial for shares.
FII / FPIforeign institutional / portfolio investor; the overseas money whose flows move Indian prices.
Floatshares actually available to trade; low float means violent prices.
FOMOfear of missing out; the buy signal generated by other people's wins (Chapter 77).
Futuresstandardised exchange-traded contracts to buy or sell later at a price fixed now; margined daily.
Gilta government security; sovereign risk in local currency is conventionally called risk-free.
Greeks (options)delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho: how an option's price moves with price, time, volatility, rates.
Hedgingpaying to reduce a risk you already own; insurance, not income.
Indexa weighted basket measuring a market: Nifty 50, Sensex; the benchmark and the default portfolio.
Inflationthe general rise in prices; the silent tax every return must outrun.
IPOinitial public offering; a company's first sale of shares to the public.
Large / mid / small capcompanies ranked by market value; SEBI fixes the bands by rank (top 100, 101-250, rest).
Leverageborrowed force: debt or derivatives magnifying gains and losses equally.
Liquidityhow fast an asset sells near its true price; abundant until needed (1987, 2020).
Marginmoney posted to support leveraged positions; margin calls are forced decisions at bad times.
Margin of safetyGraham's rule: buy materially below value so the unknown is prepaid.
Market capitalisationshare price times shares; the market's current price tag on the whole company.
Mutual fundpooled money managed professionally, bought and redeemed at NAV; Parts V and XIV.
NAVnet asset value: a fund's per-unit worth, struck daily after the close.
Nifty 50 / SensexNSE's and BSE's headline indices; India's two thermometers.
Optionthe right, not the obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) at a strike by an expiry; premium is the price of the right.
P/E ratioprice per rupee of earnings; how many years of current profit the price prepays.
PEGP/E divided by growth rate; Lynch's quick value gauge.
Pledgingpromoters borrowing against their shares; a red flag with a Chapter 81 pedigree.
Portfolioeverything you own, treated as one engineered object; allocation, drift, rebalance.
Premium (options)the option's price; the buyer's maximum loss, the seller's immediate income.
Quantum / quantitynot finance terms; check you are reading a finance book.
REIT / InvITlisted trusts owning rent-paying property or infrastructure; income vehicles of Chapter 67.
Repo ratethe RBI's policy lending rate; the first domino of every interest rate in the country.
Rights issueexisting shareholders offered new shares first, usually at a discount.
Risk-free ratethe yield on short sovereign paper; the baseline every risk must beat to be worth it.
Rupee cost averagingfixed sums buying more units when prices are low; the SIP's quiet engine.
SEBISecurities and Exchange Board of India; the market's referee and this book's recurring hero.
Sharpe ratioexcess return per unit of total volatility; the standard risk-adjusted score.
SIPsystematic investment plan; automated periodic fund buying, behaviour design by standing instruction.
Sortino ratioSharpe with only downside volatility punished; kinder to upward wildness.
Spreadthe bid-ask gap, or a yield difference over the benchmark; context decides.
SWPsystematic withdrawal plan; the SIP run backwards for retirement income.
T+1 settlementtrades settle the next business day; India's cycle, among the world's fastest.
Total return indexan index with dividends reinvested; the honest benchmark version.
Volatilitythe size of price swings; risk's most measurable costume, not its only one.
XIRRthe annualised return consistent with dated cash flows; the only honest answer for SIPs.
Yieldincome over price; for bonds, YTM is the full-picture yield to maturity.
Yield curveyields across maturities; its shape is the bond market's economic forecast.
Zero-coupon bondno coupons; bought at a discount, redeemed at face; duration equals maturity.

84.2Regulators, institutions and abbreviations

AMFIAssociation of Mutual Funds in India; the industry's standards body.
BSE / NSEBombay and National Stock Exchanges; the two trading floors (now networks) of India.
CDSL / NSDLthe two depositories holding demat shares.
EPFOEmployees' Provident Fund Organisation; runs the salaried retirement default.
FMCForward Markets Commission, the old commodities regulator, merged into SEBI in 2015.
IRDAIInsurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India.
MCAMinistry of Corporate Affairs; company law and the registrar of companies.
NCLTNational Company Law Tribunal; where insolvency cases are heard.
PFRDAPension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority; runs NPS.
RBIReserve Bank of India; central bank, rates, currency, bank supervision.
SFIOSerious Fraud Investigation Office; corporate fraud's specialist investigator.

84.3Market slang, decoded

Operatora player alleged to move prices; treat the word as an accusation, not a role.
Multibaggera stock that multiplies several times; Lynch's ten-bagger, Indian accent.
Upper / lower circuitthe day's maximum allowed move up or down; locked means no counterparty at all.
Pump and dumphype the price, sell into the believers; Chapter 81's telegraphic era variant included.
Dead cat bouncea brief recovery inside a fall; even a dead cat bounces from high enough.
Buying on dipsadding during declines; wise with a plan, a slogan without one.
Long / shortowns it, profits from rises / owes it, profits from falls.
Blue chipa large, proven, liquid company; poker's most valuable chip, borrowed.
Bag holderwhoever owns the thing after the story ends; do not be the bag holder.
Skin in the gameown money at risk in the advice being sold; check for it everywhere.
